Certificate lifetime planner

The CA/Browser Forum (Ballot SC-081) is cutting the maximum validity of publicly trusted TLS certificates in steps. If you renew a fixed number of days before expiry, this is how often renewal will happen:

The 200 → 100 → 47-day schedule

Publicly trusted TLS certificates issued on or after 15 March 2026 can be valid for at most 200 days. From 15 March 2027 the limit is 100 days, and from 15 March 2029 it's 47 days. Domain-validation reuse shrinks as well, to 10 days by 2029. Certificates issued before each date keep their original expiry. Private/internal CAs aren't bound by these rules.

At 47 days, manual renewal stops being realistic. If anything in your estate still gets certificates by hand (appliances, load balancers, legacy apps), now is the time to move it to ACME or another automated workflow.

FAQ

Is it safe to paste a certificate here?

Certificates and CSRs are public data, but internal ones can still reveal hostnames. This decoder runs entirely in your browser, and the page's Content-Security-Policy blocks outgoing network requests. Never paste a private key into any website. This tool refuses to decode them.

What is the SPKI pin?

It's the base64 SHA-256 hash of the certificate's SubjectPublicKeyInfo. It stays the same across renewals that reuse the same key, which makes it useful for pinning in mobile apps, curl's --pinnedpubkey, or checking that a CSR matches a certificate.

How do I check that a CSR matches a certificate or key?

Decode both here and compare the SPKI pin. If the pins are identical, they contain the same public key.

How is the validity period counted?

Per the CA/B Forum Baseline Requirements, validity runs from notBefore to notAfter inclusive. That's why a "200-day" certificate shows as exactly 200.0 days here.
